🎣 Thunder Bay shore-fishing forecast

how to read this · ⓘ
Two measured signals, kept visibly separate. The teal wash is temperature — the species' published preferred bands (faint = in range, brighter = optimal). It moves with the forecast, because the water does. The gold shading is structure — real drop-offs, shoal tops and point tips measured in the CHS survey soundings. Its brightness is a true gradient: how strongly that bottom ranks against the whole surveyed shore (75th→99th percentile), so flat water gets no gold and the brightest gold is genuinely rare. Each species only shows gold in water it can use (adult lakers hold ≥4 m in summer, brookies shallower — so the gold differs by species on purpose). The marks never move — the bottom doesn't. Nothing is blended: fish where bright gold sits inside bright teal — right structure in right-temperature water, read directly off two honest layers. All within a ~75 m cast (≤22 m deep). It's a ranking, not a catch percentage — that would need logged trips. The banner up top is the upwelling phase — the timing layer: a west-quadrant blow first drives cold water to shore (setup/peak — a cold shock that slows the bite), then as it relaxes the front sharpens and the bite turns on — that relaxation is the prime window. Day 0 uses observed wind (Thunder Bay airport); later days use the forecast. Edges are uncertain ~100–300 m — treat them as fuzzy. Blue teardrops are river mouths (plume + forage) — they rival temperature for shore catch, and for salmon/steelhead the plume/season drives more than temperature. The "Best today" numbers are a relative score, 0–100 — 100 is the strongest stretch today for that species (the most reachable water at the right temperature and structure); the rest are scored against it. It is NOT a catch percentage. Forecasts water, not fish — check Ontario regs before fishing.
