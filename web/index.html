🎣 Thunder Bay shore-fishing forecast

how to read this · ⓘ
Shading ranks where the fish are by combining two things the research supports: the optimal temperature for the species (published thermal preferences) and the thermal edge / drop-off (computed from how fast the modelled bottom temperature changes — fish hold and feed on these breaks). The darkest prime zone is where both line up (optimal temp on an edge); good is the optimal water or in-range water on an edge; fair is the rest of the preferred range — all within a ~75 m cast (≤22 m deep). It's a ranking, not a catch percentage: the temperature and the edge are data, combined without invented weights; local calibration comes from logged trips. The banner up top is the upwelling phase — the timing layer: a west-quadrant blow first drives cold water to shore (setup/peak — a cold shock that slows the bite), then as it relaxes the front sharpens and the bite turns on — that relaxation is the prime window. Day 0 uses observed wind (Thunder Bay airport); later days use the forecast. Edges are uncertain ~100–300 m — treat them as fuzzy. Blue teardrops are river mouths (plume + forage) — they rival temperature for shore catch, and for salmon/steelhead the plume/season drives more than temperature. Forecasts water, not fish — check Ontario regs before fishing.
